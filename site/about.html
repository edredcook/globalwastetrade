About

Ed Cook

Ed is a plastic pollution and waste management researcher specialising in circular economy in the Global South. He was a core member of the team behind the P2O model and Breaking the Plastic Wave (2020), helped build the waste component of the OECD's Global Plastics Outlook, and co-developed the SPOT model, a global plastic pollution model published in Nature (Cottom et al. 2024). He is lead author of the World Bank's What a Waste 3.0 (2026).

How the figures were created

Trade records

Annual trade for each waste code, 2008 to 2025, comes from the UN Comtrade database. Exporters' and importers' reports of the same trade often differ, so they are shown as separate views and never added together. A third, 'resolved' view uses the mean where both sides of the transaction have reported, and the one available report otherwise.

One figure per route

For each reporter, partner and direction, the total across all customs procedures and transport modes is used, taken from the partner-2 'World' record where there is one and otherwise from the sum of the detail.

Cleaning

Rule A
Missing weights are filled from the partner's report of the same trade or, failing that, from the value at the median price per tonne for that code.
Rule B
Weights whose price per tonne is more than ten times above or below the median for that code are replaced by the partner's figure, where that figure is in range.
Rule C
Other odd records are left as reported and flagged.
Rule D
Where there is no usable partner figure and the price is more than 100 times above or below the median, the weight is estimated from the value. This is not applied to refuse-derived fuel, which can move at a gate fee.
Rule E
Weights that UN Comtrade estimated for a short list of reporters and years, where the estimate is clearly wrong, are replaced by the partner's figure or by the reporter's own price per tonne in nearby years.

Imputation

'Imputed' is shown by default and can be switched off by selecting 'As reported'. Where a country has not filed for a particular year, its trade is imputed separately in each view, in this order:

  1. from its partners' records, scaled by the gap between its own and its partners' figures in its nearest reporting years (limited to between half and double);
  2. for a gap of one or two years inside a run of its own reports, on a straight line between the years either side;
  3. from its nearest own report up to three years away, moved by the change among countries that reported in both years.

Each imputed route is labelled with the method used. Territories inside another country's customs figures (such as San Marino in Italy's) are not counted as missing.

Modelled years

Many countries do not submit data to UN Comtrade consistently, and there is often a time lag before a reliable global dataset becomes available. The Global Waste Trade Data Hub therefore models recent data to give a complete and up-to-date picture of the international trade, acknowledging the shortcomings of modelled data.

The current year is shown before annual returns exist. Each country's monthly returns so far (UN Comtrade monthly data) are scaled to a full year by its own pattern in the previous year, then gaps are imputed as above. Months that EU members and the UK have not yet sent to UN Comtrade are taken from Eurostat and HM Revenue & Customs, converted to US dollars at the rate implied by months both sources hold.

These figures are provisional. The site opens by default on the most recent year in which coverage is more or less complete.

Income groups

Income groups follow the World Bank's classification for the year in which the trade takes place, so a country that moves group is counted in the group it was in at the time.

Other and embedded plastics

Many waste materials and products are composites, assemblies or mixtures. Used clothing, e-waste and refuse-derived fuel, for example, all contain plastic. Users can include these flows in the charts so that the full range of plastic in traded waste can be analysed. Their tonnages also come from UN Comtrade and are cleaned in the same way.

The quantity of plastic in each stream rests on an assumption. Each is set to the default value shown below, with its source, and can be edited by users as new information becomes available. Scientifically justified suggestions or improvements to these assumptions are welcome; please contact the site owner.
